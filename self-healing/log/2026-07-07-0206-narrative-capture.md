## Doctrine captured this session

**Decentralize-the-benefit, not the raw capability.** Core direction: decentralize the benefits of the twin/DI stack and let them flow, gated on AI-safety requirements being met. Explicit caution on the market-capital-competition side — decentralized capability landing ungated risks avoidable industry disruption and, if individuals out-run institutional adaptation, civil unrest. The stated goal is to get ahead of that gap: inverted data-collection onus builds evidence for institutional restructuring (not an increase in litigation — restructuring work for counsel, yes; adversarial litigation, not the goal), so institutions adapt before individuals route around them.

**Twin-fork gifting as community service.** Anyone who forks the base twin can hand access back (or send the fork in); ingesting it, doing the improvement pass, and returning a new version is lightweight — a few hours for any DevOps team member. Every person on the team gets the option to nominate a cause in their own life (a family member's church, a spouse's soup kitchen, etc.) and route a free/low-cost improvement pass there as their personal community-service gift. Author's framing: concrete, personal generosity — not a CSR line item — is what moves people from fearing AI to seeing it as opportunity.
