2. Three Classifications of Distressed Projects

Classification 1
Strategic Drift
Loss of target image and stakeholder commitment
What's being counted

Projects where the original business case has eroded mid-flight. Measured by gap between expected and actual value, by attrition of executive sponsorship over the program lifecycle, and by retrospective regret about scope clarity.

Statistical evidence
73% of executives: expected value significantly exceeded actual results Horváth, May 2025 (200 executives, 6 countries) 68%: stakeholder commitment drops noticeably as project progresses Horváth, May 2025 30%: wish project scope had been defined more clearly Horváth, May 2025
EVE Rescue Motion
EVE agents re-anchor the target image — replay the original business case, surface drift against it, restore executive sponsor visibility through DI CoE live-stream.
Classification 2
Scope Overload
Too many initiatives bolted onto the transformation
What's being counted

Projects where the S/4HANA transformation is carrying parallel programs — third-party system replacements, management-model redesign, digital tooling, AI initiatives — all on the same timeline. Measured by reported overload, by mid-flight scope/quality compromises, and by frequency of unresolved priority conflicts.

Statistical evidence
78% of respondents: too many topics integrated into the transformation Horváth, April 2025 68%: reduce scope or quality to meet schedule Horváth, May 2025 Parallel initiatives commonly bolted on: third-party system replacement, management-model redesign, AI tooling, digital foundations Horváth, May 2025
EVE Rescue Motion
EVE agents triage the program — must-have vs. nice-to-have, sequenced for delivery. Parallel initiatives get unbundled, not abandoned.
Classification 3
Foundation Decay
Data, change, and resource debt accumulating under the surface
What's being counted

Projects where the visible work is moving, but the foundations underneath — master data quality, change-management adoption, resource adequacy — are eroding. Measured by reported budget/resource tightness, by data quality incidents, and by post-go-live adoption shortfall.

Statistical evidence
73% of managers: budget and resources are generally too tight Horváth, May 2025 Data quality is a top failure root cause; legacy data carries forward into the new system and erodes user trust Prosci research Change management is the "single biggest failure point" for ERP projects (Deloitte); human factors matter 6× more than technical factors Prosci · Velosio / Deloitte
EVE Rescue Motion
EVE agents stand up the data quality, change management, and resource governance layer — the work the SI didn't budget for. Classified dataset hardens with every engagement.

3. The DI CoE Deployment — Pyramid Formation, Human in the Loop

Apex Senior Architect ~1 hour/day · recorded · intense
Tier 2 Solution Architects SAP DNA · BDC builders · inside perimeter
Tier 3 Domain Engineers Datasphere · SAC · ACDOCA · BW · integration
Tier 4 EVE Agents (DMZ-bound) Capture · classify · surface · never act autonomously
Capture layer: WhatsApp + Teams + Email inside the perimeter — every working session recorded, classified, and added to the operational dataset

How the pyramid runs a rescue

Human in the loop at every decision node. Agents capture, classify, and surface — they do not autonomously act on the engagement. Every meaningful decision is gated by a human at the appropriate tier.
Light touch at the apex, depth in the tiers. The senior architect operates at ~1 hour/day in recorded, intense sessions. The pyramid below executes in the gaps. This is how a distressed project can move from intake to control in two weeks without parachuting a 15-person SI team.
Capture into a perimeter we own. All sessions are captured inside the DMZ-bound perimeter (WhatsApp / Teams / Email). Phone, SMS, and LinkedIn DMs sit outside by design. Customer data does not leak into third-party model training.
Classified-data accumulation. Captured engagement data — patterns, decisions, escalations, root-cause traces — is classified and added to SteelCloud's operational dataset. The corpus hardens with every engagement.
Strategic-component automation as the corpus matures. Once a recurring rescue pattern has been captured at sufficient density, the strategic component that recognizes it can be automated — narrowing the pyramid over time as agents absorb validated patterns. Humans stay in the loop on every new pattern.
Precondition for a rescue engagement: appropriate access to materials and people. Two-week intake-to-control target. ~1 hour/day of senior-architect time. Recorded sessions, inside the perimeter, every time.

How the Chatter Meter is intended to be read

The meter is ordered top-to-bottom by strategic priority — not by statistical weight. The conversion rate sits at the top because it is the single number SAP North America is measured against. Distressed projects sits directly underneath because it is the headline number for in-flight remediation, where SteelCloud's rescue motion applies. Supporting indicators are layered below in order of cause-and-effect proximity: failure pattern, automation plateau, pace, consulting cost, budget pressure, AI rollout. The stock benchmark at the bottom is a context floor — it is what SAP S/4HANA's installed base would have looked like if growth had held to SAP's stated 33% YoY trajectory from 2018, which it visibly has not.
