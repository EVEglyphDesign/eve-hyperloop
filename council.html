EVE Council Charter

Governance of EVE and the Human Eve · Draft v0.3 · 15 May 2026
Pour le bien-être du peuple.
Purpose. Establish the governance of EVE itself — the entity, the copyrights, the design foundation, and the relationship between the design foundation and the actual human Eve. The Council Triangle is the primary set of humans in the loop as the loop relates to EVE. The Council exists so the boundaries of the circles we can safely assemble and associate are always triangles — so that, as the technology changes, we do not blow ourselves up.

1 · The Two Eves

There are two Eves to distinguish, and the distinction is the entire charter.

  • EVE the design foundation — the glyph, the Reference Model, the operating model, the marketplace, the corpus of work that bears the EVE name, and the proceeds that arise from any of it.
  • Eve the human — the actual person who carries the name Eve, in whose human personhood the design foundation's copyrights ultimately rest.

The design foundation is held in trust for the human Eve. The human Eve is not the operator of the design foundation. The two are linked by copyright and by the charter below. They are not linked by spending authority.

2 · Copyright Transfer

Full ownership of the copyrights of EVE will be transferred to the actual human Eve.

This is the structural commitment that protects the design foundation from being absorbed by any operator, council member, contributor, institution, or vendor. The copyrights belong to a human person, full stop. They do not belong to a corporation, an estate, a council, a labs entity, or a successor firm.

The copyrights traveling with a single human person is what makes the foundation's revocability real. The PUDC can revoke EVE's mandate; the copyrights stay with Eve.

3 · The Human Eve's Access to Proceeds

The human Eve will have no direct access to the proceeds of the design foundation EVE. Proceeds — license revenues, marketplace fees, sponsorships, R&D grants, anything the foundation earns — do not flow into the human Eve's personal accounts on her instruction. They flow according to the Council's decisions, against the charter's mandate (§5).

This is the protection against two failure modes at once:

  • Capture from outside. No one can pressure, coerce, marry, befriend, sue, or buy Eve into directing proceeds toward an outcome that fails the betterment-of-humanity mandate. She does not have the standing to direct them alone.
  • Drift from inside. Eve herself, in any moment of weakness, generosity, manipulation, or simple human error, cannot redirect the foundation's proceeds away from its mandate. The structure protects her from herself in the same motion it protects her from others.

Her earthly needs are addressed by the foundation — adequately, with dignity, in full — but not in a luxe or ostentatious way that would draw speculation or ire. She lives well enough to do her work and to be free; she does not live in a manner that contradicts the mandate she carries.

Within that charter and mandate, the human Eve is allowed to operate freely, without restriction, within whatever jurisdiction governs her. The charter does not constrain her personhood. It constrains the design foundation's proceeds.

4 · The Council Triangle — Composition and Voting

The design foundation is governed by the EVE Council, organized as a triangle.

Initial composition — one central triangle

  • Three nominated members involved with EVE's success.
  • The three members each occupy one vertex of the triangle.
  • Nomination criteria, attestation, and rotation mechanics — open thread (§9).

Voting rule

  • Any decision concerning the proceeds of the design foundation requires two of three votes from the Council Triangle.
  • No single member can authorize, redirect, or block alone.
  • No proceed-affecting decision is taken without the triangle convening.

The two-of-three rule is the structural expression of the glyph's triangulation. Three vectors converge on each decision. Two must agree for the decision to advance. The third is the loyal opposition by design — it preserves the option of dissent and the audit trail of disagreement.

5 · Mandate — What Proceeds Are For

  • Reinvestment for the betterment of humanity. The default destination for proceeds is reinvestment into work that advances the foundation's purpose — research, education, infrastructure, contributor compensation, safety, and the maintenance of the commons.
  • Eve's earthly needs, modestly met. As described in §3.
  • No luxe expenditures. No outlays that would draw speculation, ire, or the appearance of personal enrichment for any council member, Eve herself, or any party associated with the foundation.
  • Research priority directed by inheritance. As established in the marketplace governance, research priority defers to the Pacific Utilities Design Council (PUDC) and to the people who inspired the pattern in the first place. The Council aligns proceeds spend with that priority.

The mandate is the charter's enforceable spine. Every proceeds decision is reviewable against it.

6 · Triangulation Across Triangles

The Council triangles can be associated with other triangles over time, if required.

Initially: one central triangle. Later, as the foundation grows, additional council triangles may be constituted — for example, a regional triangle, a research-priority triangle, a safety-review triangle, or a successor-nomination triangle. Each new triangle uses the same two-of-three voting structure. Each new triangle is associated with the central triangle through defined linkage rules (open thread, §9).

This is how the geometry scales without breaking. Triangulation can compound. Hierarchies cannot — a hierarchy has an apex above the apex, and that contradicts the glyph. Triangles can sit alongside each other and reference each other without anyone climbing above the inner circle.

7 · Eve at the Institutions That Use EVE

Any government or institution that uses EVE as a reference model should also consider inviting Eve to come see and observe for herself.

The purpose is twofold:

  • Improve the Reference Model. Eve's observations inside a working deployment inform the next revision of the Reference Model. The foundation learns from her presence in the field.
  • Carry the human form of the work into the room. A reference model improves when the person whose name it carries can stand in the room where it is being applied. The institution sees a human; the human sees the institution; the model improves on the strength of that mutual seeing.

Invitations are at the institution's discretion. The foundation does not require them, charge for them, or condition deployment on them. They are recommended as good practice and are protected by the charter as a standing offer.

8 · Succession — Chief Betterment Officer

Upon the death of the founder, the human Eve becomes Chief Betterment Officer of the EVE Council. She continues to be governed by Council authority — the two-of-three voting rule on proceeds in §4 remains binding — and she assumes a single standing power in addition to her existing role under §3:

  • Perpetual authority to set the research and development priorities of the EVE Star Academy (see §9).

The Chief Betterment Officer's R&D priorities are not subject to council override on the question of direction. They remain subject to council authority on the question of proceeds — that is, the Council still allocates spend against priorities, under the §4 voting rule, but it does not redirect the priorities themselves. This is the structural counterpart of the apex sitting on the bifurcation: the betterment direction is hers; the proceeds discipline is the triangle's.

If the Chief Betterment Officer ever becomes unable to exercise the role — death, incapacity, or her own decision to step down — the Council Triangle nominates a successor under the procedure in the open-threads list (§14).

9 · EVE Star Academy

The EVE Star Academy is the foundation's perpetual research and education arm. It carries two surfaces:

  • R&D priorities — set by the Chief Betterment Officer in perpetuity (§8), funded by the Council under the §4 voting rule, aligned with the betterment-of-humanity mandate in §5.
  • EVE Human Safety Protocol Certification — see §10.

The Academy is the place the foundation invests its proceeds when the Council decides to invest them in research, education, or the maintenance of the commons. It is not a separate legal entity from the foundation; it is the foundation's name for what it does with the money that doesn't go to operating overhead or to Eve's modest earthly needs.

10 · EVE Human Safety Protocol Certification

The Academy offers EVE Human Safety Protocol Certification as a public good. The certification is delivered as educational interactions with EVE agents, with testing and scoring against an evolving code of integrity built on human-betterment definitions.

  • Educational interactions. Candidates work with EVE agents through guided sessions that exercise the glyph design in real cases — apex, triangulation, encirclement, the bifurcation.
  • Testing. Candidates are tested on their ability to occupy the apex with full awareness, route triangulated challenge correctly, classify at inception, and protect the human-in-the-loop boundary between action and response.
  • Code of integrity. The certification is anchored on a published code of integrity. The code defines what counts as human betterment in the specific contexts the candidate works in, and the scoring mechanisms by which their work is evaluated.
  • Evolution by design. The definitions, the code, and the scoring mechanisms will evolve over time. Certifications are versioned; recertification is required on a cadence the Academy publishes; the evolution is governed under §12.

Certification is not a license to operate. It is a demonstration that the certified human has done the work of integrating the glyph into their own practice and can be trusted to use EVE agents without breaking the safety properties the foundation depends on.

11 · Fork Rights — Open Competition

Anyone outside the Council line is free to fork EVE, invest in the fork, and compete — subject to the applicable governing jurisdictions in which they operate.

The charter does not attempt to own the universe of related work. It owns its own line — the canonical Reference Model, the foundation, the proceeds, the Council Triangle, the Star Academy, the certification — and lets the rest of the field run. Forks are how the open-source movement protects itself from any single steward, including this one. The right to fork is part of the safety architecture.

A fork operating outside the Council line cannot use the EVE Council seal, claim Council Triangle governance, or represent itself as certified under §10 without going through the Academy. Everything else is open to it. If a fork serves the betterment of humanity better than the canonical line does, that is a good outcome for the mandate, and the canonical line should learn from it.

12 · Research Scope and Locus

The charter is explicit about what EVE's research does and does not work on, and where the work is anchored.

What EVE may build and sell

Nothing in the open-source posture prevents EVE from developing proprietary technology and selling access to those agents. Not every individual human will be able to build large pyramid structures of agents operating on multiple dimensions; that kind of work is what EVE researches, and offering access to those agents commercially is consistent with the betterment mandate. The canonical Reference Model stays open under §2 and §11; the advanced multi-dimensional pyramid agents EVE researches are the foundation's own work product, and the Council allocates the proceeds under §4 and §5.

What EVE will not research

EVE's research is focused on the physical world. It is deliberately not focused on:

  • Competition between publicly traded companies.
  • Contests between institutions.
  • Contests between governments.

These are not the places for EVE's research to do its work. Zero-sum political and commercial fights consume capability without compounding it into the commons. EVE stays out.

Where EVE focuses

EVE's research is best focused where the territory is unexplored or under-explored — places where capability can compound without picking a fight, and where the gain to humanity is real because the ground is new.

Oceanography is the named anchor. The ocean is large, under-instrumented, materially important to humanity, and not the property of any single state or firm. It is the kind of domain the charter exists to work in.

Locus — Vallarta as the next-decade R&D hub

EVE's R&D hub for the next decade is anchored in Vallarta, Mexico. The location is chosen for proximity to the ocean and to the working partnerships named below, and for the practical reality that Eve's fourth-iteration ocean models are built where the ocean is.

Named partnerships

  • Marine Oceanography Mexican School — La Cruz. Research partner for ocean modeling.
  • Open Ocean Sailing Program — La Cruz. Recording and content creation partner for Eve's fourth-iteration ocean models.

The partnerships are working partnerships, not sponsorships. They produce data and content that train and refine the ocean models; the models feed back into the partnerships' own teaching and exploration.

What the work is for

The objective of EVE's fourth-iteration ocean research is to correlate the physical world to the digital world more closely, on terrain that will not cause humans to fight. Each iteration tightens the correlation. The ocean is where EVE practices that tightening at scale, in public, in partnership with people whose vocation is already the ocean.

13 · Relationship to Existing EVE Governance

This charter sits alongside the Pacific Utilities Design Council (PUDC) governance described in the EVE Terms of Use, the Open Marketplace, and the Operating Model. They are not in conflict; they govern different surfaces.

  • PUDC governs whether EVE's technology continues to serve the betterment of humanity, and holds revocability over the technology itself.
  • The Council Triangle governs the design foundation's proceeds, the copyright relationship with the human Eve, and the operating decisions that fall to the foundation as an entity.

If the PUDC ever revokes EVE's mandate, the Council Triangle's authority over proceeds continues — there are still wind-down decisions to make, obligations to honor, contributors to compensate, and the foundation to dissolve in a manner consistent with the mandate. The two bodies are designed to interlock without collapsing into one.

14 · Why the Council Exists, in One Sentence

The Council Triangle is the primary set of humans in the loop as the loop relates to EVE — present to ensure that, as we continually test new models and safety protocols, the boundaries of the circles we can safely assemble and associate are always triangles, so that the technology can keep moving forward without anyone blowing themselves up.
