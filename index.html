This page is a sample summary of the EVE glyph applied for the purpose of integrating nuclear human safety protocols into our decision-intelligence model. It is published as a time-stamped record of authorship and intent, following the SAP Sapphire 2026 conference. The dashboard at the foot of the page is the worked example.

EVE is a research project on classification-led decision intelligence. Its operating premise is that the durable asset in any human-supervised AI deployment is the classified base the institution owns, not the model that operates on it. The framework's role is to keep that asset on the institution's side of the perimeter — capitalisable, regulator-defensible, and protected by an operator-side non-retention guarantee — while the model layer modernises over time on the operator's side.

Applied to nuclear-adjacent governance, the same architecture produces a dashboard-shaped consumption surface that fits inside existing control-system protocols. The governing body inspects, the institution owns, the operator carries the labour. No new genre. No new oversight regime. The pattern slides into the seat the regulator already knows how to sit in.

Capability Outline

Classification Membrane
A structured layer that captures streams from the watched system — telemetry, recurring meetings, incident records, supplier exchanges — and classifies them inside a perimeter the institution owns. The classified base is the asset; the membrane is how it accrues.
Probability-Gated Escalation
Readings are reported as levels with explicit ignition probability. Escalation across thresholds requires both contextual signal and market or operational confirmation. No single-source alerting. No emergency framing absent corroborating evidence.
Industry-Agnostic Dashboard
The dashboard pattern is substrate-independent. Today's worked example watches SAP vendor-lock-in dynamics in the sovereign-AI era. The same pattern wraps reactor margin, grid stability, AI deployment density in critical infrastructure, or any other watched system the governing body chooses to point it at.
Cellular-Disarming Deployment
Each deployed instance is detachable from the operator without functional loss to the institution. No phone-home. No licensing leash. No retained rights on the classified base. The institution holds the off-switch — at year ten of asset life, not just at signing.

Integrating Nuclear Human Safety Protocols

Nuclear governance is the modern template that has successfully kept a dual-use technology operating under public-interest constraints over multi-decade horizons. Its operating elements — independent regulator with statutory authority, peer-reviewed evidence, cautious approval rather than market enablement, dashboard-shaped consumption surfaces in control rooms, and audit trails that survive personnel turnover — are precisely the elements a sovereign-AI oversight regime needs.

EVE does not propose a new governance body. It proposes that the dashboard pattern shown below is the consumption surface a nuclear-style governing body can use to extend its existing confidence across industries that today have no equivalent oversight. The governing body's confidence is the asset; the dashboard pattern is the carrier.

The dashboard pattern is the seat the regulator already knows how to sit in. The institution owns the base. The operator carries the labour. The confidence properties survive the migration from one industry to the next.

What this means in practice

  • Human safety protocols from nuclear governance are inherited as classification rules inside the membrane, not bolted on as policy
  • Escalation logic mirrors control-room conventions — probability-gated, evidence-backed, audit-trailed, conservative by default
  • The governing body retains all rights of use over the classified base accrued under its remit
  • Operator-side non-retention is structural, not contractual — see Terms of Use §3.5
